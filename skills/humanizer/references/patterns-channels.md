# Universal and channel-specific markers

Flag every hit with the exact quote and where it is. Apply the universal lists to all content, then the list for the detected channel.

## Universal phrase-level markers

- **Overused transitions**: Furthermore, Moreover, In conclusion, Additionally, It's worth noting, in summary (especially mid-post to announce a list: just give the list).
- **Hollow intensifiers**: crucial, essential, incredibly, significantly.
- **AI vocabulary**: delve, leverage (verb), transformative, game-changing, seamless, robust, synergy, best practices, thought leader, landscape, paradigm, harness, navigate, unlock, empower, streamline, holistic, tapestry, multifaceted, nuanced, foster, cultivate, facilitate, utilize, comprehensive, albeit, whilst, theater, plainly, superpower, journey, reality (as dramatic reveal), elevate, realm, essentially, certainly, overall (filler qualifier: "Overall, this approach works well"), absolutely (affirmation opener: "Absolutely, here's how..."), typically, various (vague pluralizer: name the actual things).
- **AI phrasing and metaphors** (they simulate directness, enthusiasm or authenticity, and are recycled across outputs): brutal clarity, lost the plot, painfully clear, blunt honesty, that way you can, with precision, lived experience, launching a new chapter, the energy in the room, laying the groundwork, "Here's to [noun]!", will never be the same, that promise becomes reality, ends the era of, the same tension, keeping my hands dirty.
  - "not only... but also" (e.g. "It's not only fast but also reliable"): parallelism used to fake thoroughness.
  - "here's a breakdown": cut it, give the breakdown.
  - "in the ever-evolving landscape": a grand cousin of "In today's [noun]". Filler opener.
  - "a testament to": gestures at quality without saying anything. Say what the team actually did.
  - "there is a specific kind of [magic/energy/power] that happens when": vague wonder-framing ("There is a specific kind of magic that happens when a Zoom square finally becomes a person"). Describe the real experience.
  - "Below is..." / "Below:" as a list introduction: cut the intro, start the list.
  - "such as" repeated to introduce examples: name the thing directly, use a number or a specific name.
- **Stacked abstract noun lists**: three or more abstract nouns for emotional weight ("creativity, passion, joy and drive"). Replace with one concrete claim or one noun.
- **Passive voice** where active is stronger.
- **Hedge phrases**: It's important to note that, One might argue, It goes without saying.
- **Filler openers**: In today's [noun], When it comes to, At the end of the day, The truth is (front-loads candor before a generic claim: just state the claim; distinct from "And I'll be honest:", which is a mid-sentence pivot).
- **Product-tagline phrasing** in non-product contexts ("Hands-free until review", "Built for scale").
- **Runway sentences**: vague hype lines before the real detail. Cut the runway.

## Universal structural markers

- Opens with a generic claim instead of a specific story, example or contrarian take.
- Bullet points where prose would carry more weight; the intro, 3-point list, conclusion template.
- Closes by summarizing what was just said instead of a challenge, principle or open question.
- Every paragraph about the same length.
- Stacked fragments as punchlines ("X. Y. Z."): rewrite as a real sentence.
- No concrete example, data point or firsthand experience anywhere.
- **Three-part parallel structure**: "It's not about X. It's about Y. It's about Z." One direct sentence instead.
- **Colon-list**: a mid-sentence colon list where prose reads better. Under four items, write a sentence.
- **Contrast-based negation**: "It's not X. It's Y.", "This isn't about X. It's about Y." State it positively.
- **Exclamation-point inflation**: enthusiasm the content didn't earn. Use periods.
- **Adverb-stacking pivot**: "X matters. Y matters. But that's not the point. The point is Z." One declarative sentence.
- **Declarative simplicity setup**: "The answer is straightforward:" Cut the setup.
- **Self-posed question as transition**: "Why? Because..." Make it a statement.
- **Declarative reveal**: "The skill that will separate...? It's critical thinking." Just state the claim.
- **Label-colon framework**: observations packaged as named label: description pairs to fake a framework. Prose, unless documenting a real methodology.
- **Stat bomb opener**: three or more rapid statistical fragments. Weave stats into sentences.
- **Honesty disclaimer**: "And I'll be honest:", "I'll be real:". State the opinion.
- **Credential stacking opener**: two or three credentials before the advice. Weave one in or skip.
- **Definition reframe**: a pithy formula ("It's an execution problem dressed up as a leadership problem."). State the observation plainly.
- **Punchy orphan closer**: a short standalone mic-drop line at the end. Close with a real thought.
- **Tension-colon opener**: opening on a colon-separated tension statement. Just state the observation.
- **Parenthetical asides for fake candor**: one is fine, several are performance.
- **Standalone hype fragment**: "This is big." "Game changer." Replace with a specific claim.
- **Triple rhetorical question hook**: two or three rapid rhetorical questions to manufacture intrigue. Open directly or with a specific story.
- **Reading complexity creep**: clusters of multi-syllable words and nested clauses above a 10th-grade level (LinkedIn reportedly gives such text about 35% less reach). Flag three or more 3-syllable words in one sentence, or sentences with two or more embedded dependent clauses. Aim for 7th to 9th grade in conversational professional writing.

## LinkedIn markers (only when detected as LinkedIn)

Phrase-level:
- Pivot transitions: "But here's the thing", "And here's the kicker", "Here's what most people miss", "Let me explain", "Here's why that matters".
- Engagement-bait closers: "Agree?", "Thoughts?", "What would you add?", "Drop a comment if...", "Repost if this resonates". If the post is worth engaging with, people will.
- Vulnerability performance: "I'll be honest", "Can I be real for a second?", "I wasn't going to share this but...". Real vulnerability doesn't announce itself.
- Fake humility: "I'm no expert, but...", "I don't have all the answers, but...", "This might be controversial, but...". They always precede confident claims.
- Tag-and-thank: five or more tags with "Shoutout to...". One or two genuine tags are fine.
- Dream-realized language: "I realized my dream", "A dream come true", "Pinch me moment". Say what happened.
- Arrow-chain formats (-> process flow): reads as a slide deck. Write a sentence.
- ALL-CAPS single-word injection: "HUNDREDS of customers. MILLIONS in ARR." Earn emphasis with the real number.
- "What if I told you...": raises "see more" clicks 30 to 40% but is a known ghostwriter template. Valid only when followed by a specific, non-obvious insight the author really has.
- "Here's what nobody tells you about...": signals insider access. Flag when the content under it is generic. What did the author observe that others haven't said?
- "Read that again." / "Let that sink in.": manufactured gravity after an unremarkable line (found at about 22x normal frequency in AI posts). Cut both.
- "And honestly?": fake candor before a non-controversial claim. State the opinion.

Structural:
- One-line paragraph formatting throughout: the number one AI/ghostwriter tell. Group related sentences.
- Hook, 3-point list, mic-drop closer.
- Explaining the algorithm ("Gets it into more feeds"). Just ask.
- Vulnerability-bait hook: a personal failure story used to hook, then a tidy lesson. If the story is real, leave it messy.
- "We didn't just build X. We built Y" negation upgrade. Say what you built.
- Hyperbole opener: "X will never be the same." "Everything changed." Start with the specific thing that happened.
- Common-belief-then-counter opener: "Zapier is for simple workflows. That's what most people think. And they're dead wrong." Start with the actual insight.
- Period-separated word emphasis: "every. single. day." Use normal rhythm.
- Self-intro paragraph at the bottom ("My name is X and I'm a...") on a milestone or story post. Cut it or weave the credential into the body.
- Information-withheld hook: the opening deliberately hides the subject to force the "...more" click ("At Netflix, we had 1 UNSPOKEN rule. It was about this 1 word."). State the insight first.
- "X is [positive]. [X variant] is a whole different game": "Prompt-to-prototype is magical. Prompt-to-production is a whole different game." Collapse into one direct sentence about the real challenge.
- Cliche proverb opener ("Work smarter, not harder."). Start with the observation that prompted the post.
- External link CTA ending: a URL in the body or "link in comments" reportedly kills about 60% of reach. Describe the resource and add the link in a first comment, or omit.
- Achievement post formula: emotion word + announcement, team thanks, generic lesson, emoji sign-off ("Thrilled to announce... Couldn't have done it without my incredible team. The biggest lesson: consistency wins. Excited for what's next!"). Replace with what made this milestone hard, surprising or meaningful.
- Fake dialogue format: an opinion dressed as a conversation between roles (CEO/CMO, Founder/Investor). Rewrite as direct prose with the argument first.

### Hook versus value (LinkedIn only)

The feed works in two stages. Most AI-assisted posts win stage 1 and die at stage 2.
- **Stage 1, distribution (first 30 to 60 minutes):** the hook decides whether people click "see more". Won or lost in the first 2 to 3 lines.
- **Stage 2, continued distribution:** dwell time, saves and substantive comments. Generic content has a reader finishing in 8 seconds, nothing worth saving, and "Great insight!" comments. A save is reportedly worth about 5 likes, and replies-to-replies about 2.4x a surface reaction.

Hooks that clear stage 1 and earn stage 2: a **specific consequence opener** ("I lost my best employee yesterday."); a **data point with personal stakes** (one number and what it meant); a **contrarian claim with named evidence** only this author has; a **story that ends unresolved**.
Hooks that game stage 1 and kill stage 2: information-withheld hooks, "What if I told you" and "Here's what nobody tells you" over generic content, triple rhetorical questions, ALL-CAPS intensity, cliche proverbs.

**Saves test:** is there one specific, referenceable thing (a named tool, a concrete step, a number with context, a decision the author really made) someone would save? AI posts rarely pass.
**Comment test:** is there a claim specific enough to disagree with, a tradeoff with no obvious right answer, or a story without a lesson? Generic takeaways draw "So true!". Engagement-bait CTAs are treated as spam behaviour.

(Reach figures above come from the LinkedIn analyses this list was built from, not from official documentation: treat them as directional.)

## Email markers (only when detected as email)

Phrase-level:
- AI greetings: "I hope this email finds you well", "I trust this message finds you in good spirits", "Hope you had a great weekend" (to someone you don't know).
- AI closings: "Please don't hesitate to reach out", "I look forward to hearing from you", "Thank you for your time and consideration", "Warmest regards", "With gratitude".
- Corporate filler: "I wanted to reach out because...", "I'm writing to inform you that...", "Per our previous conversation", "As per my last email", "Going forward", "At your earliest convenience", "Please be advised".
- Fake personalization: "I noticed your company is doing great things in [industry]", "I was impressed by your recent [post]". If you can't cite something specific, delete the flattery.
- Hedges: "I was wondering if perhaps...", "Would it be possible to maybe...", "I just wanted to quickly check if...".
- Jargon: circle back, loop in, touch base, sync up, deep dive, bandwidth, on my radar, double-click on, unpack.
- Politeness stacking: one "thanks" is enough.
- Throat-clearing: "I'd be remiss if I didn't mention...", "It goes without saying that...".
- Subject lines: "Quick question", "Following up", "Checking in", "A thought", "[First name], quick thought". Say what the email is about.

Structural:
- More than one ask; the ask buried at the bottom (lead with it).
- Two to three times longer than its purpose needs.
- Opens with context the recipient already knows.
- Greeting mismatched to the relationship ("Dear Mr. Smith" to someone emailed 20 times).
- Vague CTA ("Let me know if you'd like to chat sometime" versus "Free Tuesday at 2pm?").
- Reads like a template with blanks filled in; stacked sign-offs; a "PS:" that is the real pitch.

## Slack markers (only when detected as Slack)

Phrase-level:
- Over-formal: "I wanted to reach out regarding...", "Please be advised that...", "At your earliest convenience". Write like you talk.
- Corporate filler: "Just wanted to flag...", "Wanted to surface this...", "Looping in [name] for visibility".
- Hedging in a fast medium: "Sorry to bother you, but...", "I might be wrong, but...", "Not sure if this is the right channel, but...".
- Emoji overload: three or more in a short message.

Structural:
- Too long (more than 4 or 5 sentences means email, doc, or a thread with a TL;DR).
- The ask buried in the middle of a long message. Lead with it.
- Greeting + body + sign-off structure. Just say the thing.
- Explaining context the channel already has.
